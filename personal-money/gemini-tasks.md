# Tasks in Gemini

Tasks in Gemini is a mode in Google's Gemini app that can carry out tasks for you, including shopping. Google says Gemini now uses the [Agent Payments Protocol (AP2)](../glossary.md#ap2) to make purchases, and that it can check out with a Google Pay integration.[^gg-04]

This is separate from [Gemini with Google Wallet](gemini-google-wallet.md), which answers money questions from Wallet data and is read-only.

## What it can do with money

- In a 17 November 2026 blog post, Google describes checking out with the new Google Pay integration, and says Gemini now uses AP2 for purchases like that.[^gg-04]
- It says it is working with Mastercard and Visa to keep purchases secure with digital tokens on supported cards. American Express is coming soon.[^gg-04]
- When you shop with Gemini, the post says you still earn your card's points, cashback or purchase protections during checkout. Receipts and tracking info land in Google Wallet.[^gg-04]
- Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) support is described as next, not as something already live.[^gg-04]

## Who approves what

- You can set a hard spending cap up front, and Gemini is designed to stay within your budget.[^gg-04]
- Gemini is designed to ask for your confirmation or pre-approval before clicking "Buy," and to ask clarifying questions if an instruction is ambiguous.[^gg-04]
- For a routine action you already trust, you can set that to "Always allow".[^gg-04]
- You can adjust task settings, disconnect Google and third-party apps, or delete chat history at any time.[^gg-04]

The default cap amount is not in the post, so it is unknown.

## Who can use it

Tasks in Gemini is rolling out free of charge in the US, Japan, Korea, Canada, Australia and India. Google also says it is rolling out a set of advanced features, starting in the US.[^gg-04]

You can try it by switching from Chat to Task in the Gemini app, by texting on Android or iOS, or by forwarding emails to gemini@google.com.[^gg-04]

The post does not say in which countries checkout with Google Pay is available. The checkout example is a US anecdote, and advanced features are described as starting in the US, so countries for checkout beyond the US are unknown.
