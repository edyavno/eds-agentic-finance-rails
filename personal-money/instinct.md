# Instinct

Instinct is a personal assistant that works through iMessage and WhatsApp. Plaid says it powers read-only financial account connections for US users, and Stripe says Link's agent wallet helps power Instinct's payments.[^pb-09][^sp-01]

## How does it connect to your accounts?

- **Plaid.** Once you have an Instinct account, you can ask the assistant money questions in iMessage or WhatsApp. Instinct then prompts you to authenticate your accounts through Plaid for more personalized answers.[^pb-09] Plaid is a [data aggregator](../glossary.md#data-aggregator).
- **What it reads.** Plaid says Instinct reads balances and transactions to spot patterns, make recommendations and flag what needs attention, and can read accounts alongside connections like email and calendar.[^pb-09]
- **Read-only.** Plaid describes the connection as read-only.[^pb-09]

## What can it do with money?

- Plaid gives examples: splitting grocery bills with a roommate, where Instinct finds the orders, works out who owes what and messages each roommate their share; and flagging a possible duplicate charge and offering to draft a dispute, with approval asked before any next step.[^pb-09]
- Stripe says its Link agent wallet helps power payments for Instinct. [Link wallet for agents](../payments/link-wallet-for-agents.md) explains how it works.[^sp-01]
- A CNBC report says JPMorgan blocks outside agents such as Muse and Instinct from account access. See [Banks and outside agents](banks-and-outside-agents.md).[^bl-01]

## Company-reported figures

Plaid says 35% of Instinct users connected a personal credit card within three weeks, and that Instinct is one of more than 800 AI-native startups building with Plaid. These are Plaid's figures.[^pb-09]

## Unknown

- Which accounts and institutions Instinct supports.
- Whether Instinct can move money from a bank account. Plaid describes read-only access, and no source describes bank transfers.
- How many people can use it. CNBC calls it invitation-only, while Plaid says thousands of US users.
