# Institutional markets

Most of this wiki covers an agent acting for one person. This page covers agents acting for firms that trade with each other. So far it has one example: a pilot U.S. Treasury bill negotiation by BNY and Microsoft.

## What did BNY and Microsoft do?

On 2026-11-17 BNY and Microsoft announced what BNY calls an industry-first pilot trade negotiation of a U.S. Treasury bill using AI agents.[^bn-01]

- BNY says its [Agentic Negotiation Protocol](../glossary.md#agentic-negotiation-protocol-anp) (ANP), which is patent pending, enabled agents developed by BNY and Microsoft Frontier Company to negotiate securely with one another.[^bn-01]
- BNY says human traders reviewed and approved the proposed terms to complete the execution of the transaction.[^bn-01]
- Microsoft's own pages describe the same pilot as AI agents operating within defined controls and human oversight, and as the first agentic negotiation of a U.S. Treasury trade.[^mi-01][^mi-02]
- The tenor, size, price and date of the trade are unknown. The sources do not give them.

## How does the negotiation work?

BNY describes the steps this way.[^bn-01]

1. **Instructions.** Microsoft Treasury gave its agent specific trading instructions, and the agent connected to BNY through a secure agent gateway to ANP.
2. **Checks first.** BNY controls validated the counterparty, security, size, settlement terms and available pricing.
3. **Negotiation.** Once the checks passed, the agents negotiated prices within their authorized limits.
4. **Comparison.** When the agents agreed, the proposed terms were independently submitted and compared against ANP's central ledger to confirm both parties had the same understanding of the transaction.
5. **Human approval.** BNY and Microsoft traders reviewed and approved the proposed terms. Execution and booking were completed through the firms' existing systems and controls.

## What do the agents not do?

- BNY says human traders retain authority over final decisions and actions. It says agents cannot execute the trade on behalf of either firm, and trades are booked through existing internal trading systems.[^bn-01]
- BNY says participant-side controls provide guardrails through predefined rules and AI safety checks, keeping agents within tightly defined parameters based on approved pricing inputs.[^bn-01]
- BNY says the process preserves a record of who participated, the messages exchanged, control checks and human decisions, so the negotiation can be reviewed and reconstructed.[^bn-01]

This is close to the [draft, then you submit](../approval-models/draft-then-submit.md#does-draft-then-submit-exist-outside-brokers) model. The agent produces proposed terms, and a person approves them before anything is executed.

## How do the agents know who they are dealing with?

BNY says ANP verifies participants and allows them to connect securely, so the agents can exchange offers and counteroffers and confirm a shared understanding of the proposed terms.[^bn-01] See [Agent identity](agent-identity.md#how-do-agents-from-different-firms-identify-each-other).

## What is next?

BNY says it plans to continue testing ANP, focusing on expanding beyond the initial bilateral use case and evaluating additional negotiated workflows. It says future applications could include other transaction types such as repo and other contractual negotiations.[^bn-01]

## What is still unknown?

- The tenor, size, price and date of the trade.
- Whether other firms will join ANP, and when. BNY gives no dates.
- Whether ANP will be published for others to use. BNY describes it as patent pending and gives no documentation link.[^bn-01]
